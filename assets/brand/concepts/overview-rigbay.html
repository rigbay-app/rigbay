Rigbay: three concepts

Flat, one idea each. Ink #1F2430 (light) / #EEF1F8 (dark), accent Bay Violet #7A5AF8 (stands in for the Windows accent; not green, red or amber, which carry state in the app). Real pixel sizes below are unscaled screenshots.

A · Rack

abstract, pictorial-lite

The bay rack the dashboard already is: two empty bays and one that holds a module. Outline means empty or off, solid means occupied, the same language as the module rows.

Risk: Three bars is the most common abstract shape in tech (charts, equalisers, pause). It is the least ownable of the three.

RECOMMENDED

B · Dock

abstract, one-gesture

A bay seen from the front: a module block being lowered into an open slot. Says “plug a module in” with one gesture and no letters.

C · R-bay

letterform

A rounded R whose leg is the module: stem and bowl in ink, leg in the accent. The initial helps recognition at 16 px; the lit leg is the ownable part.

Risk: A single letter R is common (Rider, Razer, Redis, R). Ownable only through the lit leg and the pill terminals. In the lockup the R repeats the r of the wordmark.

Recommendation: B · Dock. It is the only one of the three that says what Rigbay does (a module goes into a bay) without letters or a generic bar shape, and it keeps one clear accent element that can follow the Windows accent colour. A is the safe continuation of today’s title-bar mark; C is the strongest at 16 px but the least ownable. All three use the same stroke, pill ends and accent rule, so the module marks below pair with any of them.
